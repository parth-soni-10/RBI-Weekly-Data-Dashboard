About

India's weekly reserves, in one readable place

The RBI Weekly Dashboard reads the Reserve Bank of India's Weekly Statistical Supplement and puts the reserves, gold, rupee and market series on one page — with each figure's reporting date attached, so a weekly observation is never mistaken for a live quote.

  • MaintainerParth Soni
  • StatusIndependent, not RBI
  • CadenceWeekly, after Friday's WSS
  • CostFree, no account

Who runs it

It is a personal project by Parth Soni, built and maintained independently. It is not published, operated or endorsed by the Reserve Bank of India, and nothing here should be cited as an official RBI figure. The source is public at github.com/parth-soni-10/RBI-Weekly-Data-Dashboard, so every figure on the page can be traced back to the parser that produced it.

What it shows

  • The latest week — total reserves, foreign currency assets, gold, SDRs and the IMF reserve position, in USD and in rupees, with each move against the previous Friday.
  • Markets for the same week — USD/INR, EUR/INR, NIFTY 50 and BSE SENSEX, each from its own dated series rather than derived from the reserves.
  • History — a ten-week table, 10-, 26- and 52-week chart ranges, a searchable table and a CSV of the whole fetched history.
  • Comparison — any two WSS Fridays side by side, with absolute and percentage moves, in a link you can send.
  • Context — FII/FPI flows, the 10-year G-Sec yield, REER, RBI's net forward book, monthly indirect-tax receipts, crude import values, a World Bank external-debt ranking and a market Fear & Greed composite.
  • PM CARES — the Fund's audited receipts and payments, transcribed with the Fund's own caveats rather than presented as budget spending.

Where the numbers come from

Every section names and links its source. In short: the RBI Weekly Statistical Supplement for the weekly reserves and components; the FBIL reference rate for USD/INR; NSE and BSE historical index data; NSDL for FII/FPI flows; RBI DBIE and the RBI Bulletin for G-Sec, REER and the forward book; PPAC for crude imports; PIB/CBIC monthly releases for indirect taxes; World Bank for external debt; and the PM CARES Fund's audited statements for that section.

Market quotes for the crude benchmarks come from Yahoo Finance through this site's own server-side function. Nothing is scraped from your browser.

How it stays current

A scheduled GitHub Actions run fires on Friday at 22:00 UTC — after the week's WSS is published — regenerates the committed data file and the auxiliary series, and commits what changed; that commit is what deploys. The dashboard's Reload data button additionally live-checks RBI for a week newer than the committed file and merges it in memory. A failed scrape leaves the last good snapshot in place and marks the tile, rather than replacing a number with a guess.

Observation date versus generation time

These are deliberately separate everywhere in the dashboard and in the JSON: the observation date is the Friday the WSS reports (or the month/quarter for a macro series), while fetched_at and snapshot_updated_at are when this site built the response. Comparing the two is how you can tell a quiet week from a stale one.

What it is not

It is not an RBI publication, a data vendor, an investment adviser or a real-time feed. No affiliation with or endorsement by the Reserve Bank of India is claimed, and no redistribution licence is asserted over the underlying data — consult each provider's terms before reusing a figure. Where an upstream page changes shape and a scrape fails, the dashboard keeps the last known value and labels it a fallback rather than presenting a guess as fresh data.

Found a figure that disagrees with the source? A correction is the most useful thing you can send — see Contact for what to include. The code is public at github.com/parth-soni-10/RBI-Weekly-Data-Dashboard.
